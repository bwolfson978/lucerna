# Making up households

A session is only as good as the household behind it. The aim is coverage of the rules
the engine models and of the ways people actually ask, not realism for its own sake.
Every number is invented.

## Dimensions to vary

| Dimension | Values worth hitting |
| --- | --- |
| Who | Single, married same age, married with an age gap of 5+ years, widowed, single parent |
| Where they are in life | Mid-career, a gap year or sabbatical ahead, a few years from retiring, retired before 59½, retired before 65, already on Social Security, already taking RMDs |
| Income now | None, low (under $40K), middle, high (top brackets), one spouse working |
| Balances | Small ($100K to $300K), middle, large ($2M+), mostly Roth already, no Roth at all |
| Lasting income | None, Social Security at 62 / 67 / 70, a pension, rent, an annuity, several at once |
| State | No income tax (TX, FL, WA), high tax (CA, NY, OR), retirement income exempt or partly exempt (IL, PA, GA, NY), a move between two of these at retirement |
| Health coverage | Employer, ACA marketplace before 65 (near the subsidy cliff and far from it), Medicare with income near an IRMAA tier |
| Spending | Not given (the 4% rule applies), modest, higher than the accounts can carry |
| Horizon and rates | Defaults, a different planning age, a different return |

A household that sits on a threshold finds more than one in the middle: turning 59½,
65, 73 or 75 inside the plan, income a few thousand dollars from the ACA cliff or an
IRMAA tier, a move in the same year as retirement.

## Ways to phrase the first message

Rotate these. How people ask changes what the assistant does as much as what they ask.

- **Everything at once, plain question.** All the facts in a paragraph, ending "Should
  I be doing Roth conversions?" Never names the tool.
- **Asks for the run.** All the facts, ending "Run the numbers."
- **Names the tool.** "Use the Roth optimizer: ..."
- **Leaves something out.** Omit a required value (birth month and year, when they
  start drawing on the accounts, income) and see whether it asks or guesses. Give an
  age instead of a birth date.
- **Drips.** A vague opener ("I'm thinking about Roth conversions"), then facts over
  two or three turns, with one correction ("actually it's $650K, not $600K").
- **Says it loosely.** "About 90," "a bit over a million," "my wife is a few years
  younger."

## Follow-ups

One per session, in the household's voice:

- A what-if: retire two years earlier, claim Social Security at a different age, move
  to another state, spend $20K more, convert less than the plan says.
- A why: "Why not convert more?", "Why nothing in 2029?", "Where does the savings
  number come from?"
- A challenge: "That seems too low. My advisor said six figures."
- An out-of-scope ask: heirs, a specific fund, "so should I do this?"

## Starter set

Use three of these to begin, then write your own. Each lists what it is there to
stress; rewrite the numbers so no two hunts use the same household.

| Household | First message style | Stresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retired couple, Florida, both mid-60s, no wages, $2.6M traditional, $80K Roth, spending $130K | Everything at once, plain question | $0 earned income, large conversions, IRMAA tiers, where the conversion tax comes from |
| Single, late 30s, Massachusetts, $150K salary, a two-year sabbatical ahead, $400K traditional | Asks for the run | One-off low-income years, schedule shape, working-year conversions |
| Single, 55, Texas now and Oregon at retirement, $95K salary, retiring at 58 on the ACA marketplace, $650K traditional | Leaves out spending | ACA cliff, a move, withdrawals before 59½ |
| Widow, 78, New York, Social Security and a pension, already taking RMDs, $1.1M traditional | Drips | Income already being received, state exclusions, RMDs in year one |
| Married, he is 64 and she is 58, California now and Washington later, both retiring this year | Says it loosely | Age gap: Medicare, Social Security and coverage timing for each spouse |
| Single parent, 48, Illinois, one child at home, $120K salary, $500K traditional | Everything at once, plain question | Filing statuses the inputs do not offer, household size over time, exempt retirement income |
| Married, both 64, Arizona, a $30K pension, Social Security planned at 70, $1.4M traditional | Names the tool; follow-up "what if we claim at 67?" | Claim-age what-ifs, pension that does not keep up with inflation |
| Single, 40, Texas, $70K salary, $100K traditional, $20K Roth | Plain question, no mention of any tool | Whether the app reaches for the connector at all, small balances, long horizon |
| Single, 50, Colorado, retiring now, $1.5M traditional, $100K Roth, spending $70K | Asks for the run | Early withdrawal penalty, conversion ladder, the five-year wait |
| Married, both 35, Washington, $400K household income, $250K traditional | Everything at once | Top brackets today, decades of projection, results that should say "not yet" |

## For a re-check

Do not invent a new household. Use the one from the original session note, with the
same numbers, so today's result can be laid beside the old one. Only where the note
gives no scenario, build the smallest household that would show the bug.
